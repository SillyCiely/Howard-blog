What is Endoscopy?

Camera test to check your stomach and intestines.

A gastroscopy examines the upper digestive tract (oesophagus, stomach, and duodenum) via the mouth, while a colonoscopy examines the large bowel via the rectum. Both are day procedures performed under light sedation. Dr. Howard Fan (GESA accredited) uses these endoscopic techniques to investigate symptoms like reflux, abdominal pain, or changes in bowel habits.

02

Colonoscopy Bowel Preparation Instructions

  1. 72 hours before
    Low-residue dietAvoid nuts, seeds, grains, and leafy vegetables for 72 hours (3 days) before your colonoscopy.
  2. 24 hours before
    Clear fluids onlyClear fluids only for 24 hours (1 day) before your colonoscopy. As a rule, a clear fluid is any fluid that you can see through (this includes: water, apple juice, lemonade, black coffee, and black tea).
  3. The night before
    Bowel preparationStart your bowel preparation solutions the night before your colonoscopy. This involves 2-3 sachets of bowel preparation solution (Phosphoprep, ColonLYTELY, Glycoprep, Bisalax, Durolax, Picolax, or Picoprep). You may require extra sachets if you have experienced difficulty with adequate bowel preparation in previous colonoscopies. The expected result is to have clear fluid passing into the toilet by the time of your colonoscopy.

Advice provided on this webpage is general in nature. Your specific personal circumstances and post operative instructions may differ.
